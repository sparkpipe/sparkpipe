How it works

Your hardware, your prices, your schedule.

01

Register

sparkpipe provider register binds the installation to your account and joins it to the SparkPipe tailnet. It opens no port to the internet.

02

Offer

Pick the resident models to sell, your price per million input and output tokens, and limits on concurrency, context length and hours. One switch turns reselling on or off.

03

Serve

Network requests run at the lowest priority, on capacity your own traffic leaves idle, and yield at the next frame boundary when it arrives. A request still running finishes within the router's bound or goes back to the router.

04

Get paid

Every completion carries a receipt signed by your agent. Buyers pay your price with nothing added, and you are paid 85% of it after the challenge window.

One fabric

Interchangeable providers, one model name.

Every provider is validated, so every offer of a model at a given quantization is interchangeable. The router, a LiteLLM proxy, treats each offer as a deployment of that model at its provider's price. It spreads a buyer's requests across as many providers as it needs, by price, latency, load and verification record, and keeps requests that share a prompt prefix on one provider so its KV cache is reused. Providers, the router and the reference nodes share one tailnet.

Validation

Every provider is validated. No exceptions.

Nothing a provider's machine reports about itself is trusted. About 2% of served tokens, drawn secretly from real completed requests, are computed again on reference nodes that run the same driver build and weights on the same hardware type. Buyers get the model they pay for, and honest providers are not undercut by a quantized copy.

Greedy requests compare token for token.

Sampled requests compare logprobs, which quantization shifts even when the tokens agree.

Fingerprints and envelopes: trigger prompts only the exact weights answer, and qualified speed ranges, add cheap checks.

A mismatch holds the payout and triggers an independent re-execution. A confirmed one forfeits the provider's bond.

The replays are paid for out of sparkpipe.ai's 15%.

Questions

Before you sign up.

Can I take the hardware back whenever I want?

Yes. Your own traffic always runs first, and switching reselling off stops new network work at once. Requests already running either finish within the router's bound or go back to the router, which resumes them elsewhere.

Which models can I sell?

Any resident model your installation serves with a released SparkPipe driver. Large models pay best per token, and few installations can hold them, so fewer providers share their demand.

Who can reach my installation?

Only the router and the reference nodes, over the tailnet. Its access rules let them reach your model API's port and nothing else, and buyers only ever see a model name at sparkpipe.ai. The provider that serves a request does see its prompt, as with any hosted API: GB10 has no confidential-computing mode.

Why is validation mandatory for everyone?

Buyers pay for a specific model at its published precision. Without replays, a provider could serve a quantized copy and charge for the real one, and honest providers would be undercut. Validating every provider keeps every token on the network worth what it costs.

What hardware is supported?

DGX Spark installations of 4, 8 or 16 Sparks. DGX Station and Mac Studio support are on the roadmap. Each hardware type gets its own reference nodes, because different kernels produce different bits.

When does it open?

The network is milestone M9 on the public roadmap. It opens once batched serving reaches its throughput targets, because that is what makes reselling pay.
