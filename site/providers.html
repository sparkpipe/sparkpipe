How it works

Four steps

Your hardware, your prices, your hours.

  1. Register

    sparkpipe provider register binds the installation to your account and joins it to the SparkPipe tailnet.

  2. Offer

    Pick the resident models to sell, your price per million input and output tokens, and limits on concurrency, context and hours. One switch turns reselling on or off.

  3. Serve

    Network requests run at the lowest priority on capacity your traffic leaves idle, and yield at the next frame boundary when it arrives.

  4. Get paid

    Each completion carries a receipt signed by your agent. Buyers pay your price with nothing added; you are paid 85% of it after the challenge window.

One fabric

Interchangeable offers

Many providers behind one model name.

Every provider is validated, so every offer of a model at the same quantization serves the same model. The router is a LiteLLM proxy that treats each offer as a deployment of that model at its provider's price. It spreads a buyer's requests over as many providers as needed, by price, latency, load and verification record, and keeps requests that share a prompt prefix on one provider so its KV cache is reused.

Example: one buyer's 64 requests for GLM 5.3 Flash spread across three providers. Each provider sets its own price.

Validation

Every provider

Every provider is validated. No exceptions.

Nothing a provider's machine reports about itself is trusted. About 2% of served tokens, drawn secretly from real completed requests, are computed again on reference nodes that run the same driver build and weights on the same hardware type. Buyers get the model they pay for, and honest providers are not undercut by a quantized copy.

Greedy requestsCompared token for token.
Sampled requestsCompared on logprobs, which quantization shifts even when the tokens agree.
Cheap checksWeight fingerprints, prompts only the exact weights answer correctly, and qualified speed ranges.
On a mismatchThe payout is held and an independent re-execution decides. A confirmed mismatch forfeits the provider's bond.
Who paysThe replays come out of sparkpipe.ai's 15%.

Questions

Before you sign up.

Can I take the hardware back whenever I want?

Yes. Your own traffic always runs first, and switching reselling off stops new network work at once. Requests already running either finish within the router's bound or go back to the router, which resumes them elsewhere.

Who can reach my installation?

Only the router and the reference nodes, over the tailnet. Its access rules let them reach your model API's port and nothing else, and buyers only ever see a model name at sparkpipe.ai. The provider that serves a request does see its prompt, as with any hosted API: GB10 has no confidential-computing mode.

Why is validation mandatory for everyone?

Buyers pay for a specific model at its published precision. Without replays, a provider could serve a quantized copy and charge for the real one, and honest providers would be undercut. Validating every provider is also what makes providers interchangeable.

Which models can I sell?

Any resident model your installation serves with a released SparkPipe driver. Large models pay best per token, and few installations can hold them, so fewer providers share their demand.

What hardware is supported?

DGX Spark installations of 4, 8 or 16 Sparks. Mac Studio support (M5 Ultra) is planned, on its own or alongside Sparks. Each hardware type gets its own reference nodes, because different kernels produce different bits.

When does it open?

The network is milestone M9 on the public roadmap. It opens after batched serving lands (milestone M2), because batch throughput is what makes reselling pay.
