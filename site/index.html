How it works

Four steps

Account, HC, use, release.

  1. Open an account

    Give your company's legal name, or your own name, and a work email, confirm the email with a 6-digit code, and accept the SparkPipe Customer Agreement and the Hour Market Rules. The email is your account: no password and no crypto wallet. Every business is screened against sanctions lists.

  2. Buy HC

    Pay any amount from $10 in advance: Korean businesses in won, by bank transfer against our electronic tax invoice; customers abroad in USDC or USDT, through an invoice from our payment processor. Korean VAT of 10% is added. Once the payment has cleared it becomes HC at $20 each, to six decimal places. Each HC lasts 12 months from purchase, and the oldest is spent first.

  3. Use it three ways

    In the shared pool, open now, each request to our OpenAI-format API pays for the seconds it holds a seat, at a posted price, on fleets nobody has reserved. Dedicated fleet-hours give you a whole fleet: reserve them on the Hour Market calendar from 1 December 2026, up to 7 days ahead at the fleet's Rate in HC an hour, up to 31 days ahead at 1.25 times that on any day, or further ahead when someone releases one. No reservation is ever bumped. A standing bid fills a recurring window, such as weekdays 09:00 to 18:00, every week at no more than your price. You create the API keys, so you decide who uses them.

  4. Release what you won't use

    A reservation takes its HC at once and is final, but you may release an hour before it starts, at its Rate or a lower minimum. If someone reserves it, or the shared pool uses it when it starts, you get HC for what it earned, less a 10% fee, usable for 12 months. We never buy a released hour: it earns only what buyers and the pool pay for it.

Won payments go straight to HYOR AI Tech Ltd.'s Korean bank account. USDC and USDT payments go through a payment processor. HC cannot be sent between accounts or paid out, and a released hour passes through us to whoever reserves it, never directly between accounts. Try every step without money in the sandbox, on a test clock.

Technology

Open source

Sixteen small boxes, one frontier model.

Decoding a large model is limited by how fast its weights can be read. SparkPipe splits every weight and every cache entry across the sixteen nodes, so each reads only its own sixteenth, then merges the partial results over a 100 Gb/s RDMA mesh in 16 to 38 microseconds. The engine is written from scratch in C and CUDA and published under the Apache 2.0 license.
