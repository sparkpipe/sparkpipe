01 Why

Your time, not tokens

Per-token APIs bill you for your codebase again and again.

The API cache is built for short sessions. On Claude it lasts 5 minutes by default; a 1-hour cache costs twice the input price to write, and a 5-minute write costs 1.25 times. Kimi's default is also 5 minutes. So every break, meeting, branch switch and subagent pays for the whole context again, and waits while it prefills. That is why a $20–50 top-up runs out so fast in agentic work, and why subscription limits arrive before lunch.

03 Functions

Work on your code

Your fleet works on your code when you tell it to.

A Function is software we build and run on a fleet you hold. Start one, or schedule it, from your account, the API or the CLI. It does its own setup: the first one you run ingests your repository. Results land in your storage and your morning report, and what a Function produces is yours. We keep adding Functions.

Your state

Ingest a repository
Clones or syncs your repo, follows your pushes, and maps files, symbols and history.
Build and refresh the cache
Prefills the parts that change least first; after each push it recomputes only what changed.
Prefill ahead
Keeps the branches and modules you name warm, and prefills the likely next ones between your turns.
Resume sessions
Keeps yesterday's agent sessions warm, so you pick up where they stopped.
Export and restore
Takes your storage, cache snapshots, draft model and adapters to any fleet you hold.

Find problems

Review the day's changes
Reviews each change against the whole codebase, not just the diff, and confirms each finding with a test.
Second opinions
Runs the same review with other models and marks where they agree.
Security audit
Memory safety, concurrency, injection, secrets and dependencies, ranked, with reproductions.
Tests and fuzzing
Writes and runs unit tests and fuzzers for changed code, and reports any crash.

Do the work

Overnight agent
Works through a list of tasks and leaves a branch per task, with its test results.
Codebase-wide change
A rename, an API migration or a port, file by file, as one built and tested branch.
Ask the whole codebase
Runs one question or instruction over every file or module and collects the answers.
Keep docs current
Architecture notes, API docs and a map of the code, updated with each push.
Morning report
Everything the night produced, on one page and in one API call.

Models

Train a draft model
Trains on your repo and your sessions, so speculation accepts more of the model's tokens on your new code, week by week.
Evaluate models on your code
Runs your tests or eval set against every offered model and your own, to pick the best one per task.
Qualify your model
Checks your own model and driver against your reference outputs.
Train an adapter
Trains an adapter for an offered model or your own on your data.
Make training data
Generates training data for your own model, where each source model's license allows it.

Functions that build or run your code do it in a sandbox on your fleet, with no network unless you allow it. When a fleet changes hands we restore it to our own image, so nothing one customer ran can reach the next.

04 Your model

Private model list

Trained your own model? Serve it on your own fleet.

  1. Upload

    Send your weights to your storage with the CLI or the API. Any model that fits the fleet's memory works; Kimi K3's 1.56 TB fits.

  2. Add a driver

    We publish the model-family interface and a guide written for coding agents, so your agent can write the driver. Most new models reuse a family schedule that already exists.

  3. Check it

    A Function runs your model against your reference outputs on your fleet and reports whether they match: the same qualification our own models pass.

  4. Use it

    It appears in your model list only, next to the offered models, behind the same endpoint, and works with every Function.

05 How it works

Four steps

Demo hour, then a fleet of your own.

  1. Open an account

    Your company's legal name or your own, a work email confirmed with a 6-digit code, and the Customer Agreement. No password. Then buy HC in any quantity.

  2. Book a demo hour

    The whole demo fleet by the hour, up to 7 days ahead, 1 HC an hour. Point your own tools at it and try your codebase.

  3. Pin a fleet

    Book a first week on an available fleet, from the next Monday. When every fleet is held, join the waitlist by locking at least 144 HC.

  4. Keep it

    Book the next week before 00:00 on the Sunday of your last booked week and the fleet stays yours. The more weeks you hold ahead, the bigger the discount on every week you book, up to 20%.

The waitlist goes biggest lock first, then earliest. Fleets are allocated every Sunday at 00:00 KST, and your whole lock books as many weeks as it pays for. You see your own position and how many are waiting, nothing more. If you stop booking, the fleet goes to the waitlist that Sunday, and your cache and storage are deleted when the next customer starts.

Weeks run Monday 00:00 to Sunday 24:00 KST, and Sunday is the free day. Eight fleets at launch.

09 Technology

Open source

Sixteen small boxes, one frontier model.

Decoding a large model is limited by how fast its weights can be read. SparkPipe splits every weight and every cache entry across the sixteen nodes of a fleet, so each reads only its own sixteenth, then merges the partial results over a 100 Gb/s RDMA mesh in 16 to 38 microseconds. The engine is written from scratch in C and CUDA and published under the Apache 2.0 license.
