Questions

Before you pay

Good to know.

Do I need a crypto wallet?

No. Your account is your email. Pay by card, bank transfer, virtual account or Korean easy pay in won through Toss Payments, or by USD bank transfer (SWIFT) from your company's account outside Korea, with no wallet at all. If you pay in USDC or USDT instead, you pay on CoinPayments' checkout page from any wallet or exchange account that can send them: copy the address, or scan the QR code with your phone.

Which cards and accounts work?

Cards issued in Korea, and Visa, Mastercard and JCB cards issued abroad; bank transfer and virtual accounts at Korean banks; and the Korean easy-pay wallets shown at checkout. All are charged in won, at the won HC Price for the quantity you buy. A card issued abroad is converted to your own currency by your card issuer, and its conversion and foreign-transaction fees are yours (Customer Agreement). Toss Payments is the payment gateway; HYOR AI Tech Ltd. is the seller.

Which coin and network should I choose?

The ones you hold. Each tile shows the name exchanges use on their withdrawal screens: ERC20, TRC20, BEP20, Base, SOL, Arbitrum One or Polygon PoS. Network fees are usually cents on all of them. Send only the coin you chose, only on the network you chose: the checkout page shows the exact amount and address for it. If your exchange takes a withdrawal fee from the amount, add it on top so the whole amount arrives.

Why won, dollar stablecoins or SWIFT?

The HC Price is set in both currencies: in won when you pay in won, in dollars otherwise. A company outside Korea can pay dollars by USD bank transfer (SWIFT) straight to our US dollar account at a Korean foreign exchange bank, at 0% VAT. Other dollar payments are in stablecoins because they keep their value between the invoice and the payment, so neither of us carries the price swings of other crypto. We list USDC and USDT, which carry almost all stablecoin payments; CoinPayments' checkout page also takes the other dollar stablecoins it offers.

What can I do with HC?

Book the whole demo fleet by the hour from 1 December 2026, up to 7 days ahead, and weeks on a fleet pinned to you from 4 January 2027, in your account. Each purchase lasts 12 months unless you book with it, and you can book up to 52 weeks ahead, so a large purchase can carry you well beyond a year. Once confirmed, a booking is yours: no one can bump it.

How do weeks and the waitlist work?

A week runs Monday 00:00 to Sunday 24:00 KST and pins the fleet to you: nobody else can book it. Book the next week before 00:00 on the Sunday of your last booked week and it stays yours. When every fleet is taken, lock at least 144 HC in the waitlist: every Sunday at 00:00 KST, each fleet that comes free goes to the biggest lock, and the whole lock books your weeks from the next Monday. You can add to your lock or leave at any time, and you see only your own position.

Can I try it first?

Yes. Book a demo hour for 1 HC. Or use the sandbox with no money: buy HC, book hours and weeks, join the waitlist and call the API against a simulator, on a test clock. The sandbox checkout takes card payments in Toss Payments' test mode, where no card is charged.

Why is VAT added?

We are a Korean company, and Korean VAT of 10% applies to every payment for HC, in won or in stablecoins (Customer Agreement). The one exception is a USD bank transfer (SWIFT) from a company with no place of business in Korea, paying from its own account outside Korea: it is zero-rated, so its VAT is 0%. For a card payment, the card slip Toss Payments issues is the VAT document; for a bank transfer or virtual account in won, we issue an electronic tax invoice; a stablecoin invoice, and the invoice we email for a SWIFT transfer, show the VAT separately.

Can we get an electronic tax invoice?

Yes, for a bank transfer or a virtual account: enter your Korean business registration number at checkout, and we issue an electronic tax invoice (세금계산서) for the payment. For a card payment, the card slip is the VAT document instead, as Korean VAT law provides.

What if a payment falls short, or comes too late?

In USDC or USDT: if less arrives than the invoice, the HC it covers appears in your account at once, in proportion to what arrived, to six decimals; buy more whenever you like. Anything sent above the invoice, or after the payment page has closed, CoinPayments refunds with an email link to claim it. With a virtual account, deposit the exact amount by the deadline shown: an account not paid in time expires with its order, and nothing is owed. By USD bank transfer (SWIFT), the whole invoice total must arrive: choose charges OUR, and if bank charges take part of it on the way, send the rest with the same reference; your HC is issued once the whole amount is in, and anything above the total goes back to the account it came from.

Can a payment be refunded?

Payments are final, except where the Customer Agreement provides a refund, for example if we stop offering HC. A refund goes back the way you paid: to your card, to your bank account, by SWIFT to the account a USD bank transfer came from, or through CoinPayments' refund procedure.
