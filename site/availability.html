        How fleets are won, kept and lost

        1. Two halves to every pair

          Fleets come in pairs of 16 Sparks each, on one 32-port 100 Gb/s switch. The A half is rented by the week. The B half is an Hour Fleet: anyone can book it by the hour, up to 7 days ahead and 24 hours a week per account.

        2. Winning an A fleet

          If an A fleet is free next week, the first account to book it pins it from Monday. If every A fleet is held, you join the waitlist by locking at least 144 HC. Every Sunday at 00:00 KST, each A fleet whose holder did not book the next week goes to the biggest lock, and at equal locks to whoever joined first. That whole lock then books as many weeks as it pays for.

        3. Keeping it

          A pinned fleet is yours alone: nobody can book its hours or its future weeks. You keep it by booking the next week before 00:00 KST on the Sunday of your last booked week, and auto-extend does that for you. No booking is ever bumped, by another customer or by us.

        4. Losing it

          Miss that Sunday deadline and the fleet goes to the waitlist that night, or to the first account that books it. Your cache and storage stay until the next customer's first hour starts, and are then wiped.

        5. Adding the pair

          Holding an A fleet lets you also pin its B half for the same weeks: one cluster of 32 Sparks with 4 TB of memory. At least a quarter of the fleets must stay open for hours, so only half of the B halves can be pinned at a time. The first holder to pin one gets it, and keeps it by extending, like any fleet.

        6. Discounts for commitment

          The more weeks you hold on one fleet, the less every new week and every hour costs: 5% from 4, 10% from 13, 15% from 26 and 20% at 52. On the waitlist, your hours get the discount your lock would get, repaid from your lock if you leave before it wins a fleet.

        What it means when fleets are scarce

        Every renter plays by the same rules on the same board. These are the consequences worth planning for.

        • Fleets do not come back by themselves. A holder who keeps booking keeps the fleet for as long as they like. Once every A fleet is held, the only way in is the waitlist, and the waitlist goes to the biggest lock, not the earliest.
        • A bigger lock wins sooner and pays less per week. When you win, your whole lock books weeks at the discount for that many weeks, so a 26-week lock both outranks a 4-week lock and buys 15% off every week.
        • Booking ahead is your protection. Nobody can take a week you have booked. The only way to lose a fleet is to let a Sunday deadline pass, so keep several weeks booked ahead with auto-extend, and keep enough HC for it.
        • Pairs are the scarcest thing on the board. Only half of the B halves can be pinned at once. If you will need 4 TB, pin your pair while one is open; once you hold it, nobody can take it while you extend.
        • Hours are first come, first served. They open 7 days ahead and are capped at 24 a week per account, so no team can take them all, and at least a quarter of the fleets always stay open for hours.
        • Everyone sees the same board. It shows what is held and until when, how many locks are waiting and how many pairs are open, never who. Your own place on the waitlist is on your account page.

        The SparkPipe Customer Agreement sets these rules (clauses 14 to 16), and the Fleet List lists the fleets.
