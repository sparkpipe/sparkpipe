A 1M-token Kimi K3 prompt, 76 minutes from cold today, is estimated at about 17 minutes with four accelerator boxes and 9 minutes with eight. Fleet 1 and Fleet 2 together hold 4 TB, and Fleet 3 adds 2 TB more, so models above 2 TB run across two fleets: Qwen 3.8 Max at its published FP8 precision (2.5 TB) is estimated at 30 tok/s across Fleets 1 and 2, and Inkling at its published BF16 precision (1.9 TB) fits the same way.

How the estimates are made:

  • Decode is limited by how fast each node reads its share of the weights a token uses. Fleet 1 is taken at 80% of that limit (Kimi K3 reaches 58% today), Fleet 2 at 70% of its limit, with the model split over all eight Mac Studios, which are joined by 50 Gb/s links plus 10 GbE.
  • Speculation on agent coding turns is taken as 2.5 times plain decode, between the 1.4 to 1.6 times measured on short edits and the 8.3 times measured on whole-file rewrites.
  • Prefill accelerators are boxes of four RTX 5080 cards that reduce over PCIe inside the box and reach the fleets at 100 Gb/s each over a 400 Gb/s switch. Each long prompt streams the model's weights through the boxes once (50 GB/s with four boxes, 100 GB/s with eight), and the cards compute at 40% of their dense peak (about 450 TFLOPS each in FP8, 225 in BF16), including the attention cost of the prompt. Prompts shorter than about 20,000 tokens stay on the fleet's own prefill.
  • Qwen 3.8 Max at its published FP8 precision (2.5 TB) is larger than one fleet's 2 TB, so today its experts stream from NVMe at 10.4 tok/s. Its 4-bit builds (AMD MXFP4, Red Hat and RadixArk NVFP4) fit one fleet once one passes our accuracy A/B.

Model licenses

Each model is the publisher's open-weight software under its own license, which the customer must follow (Hourly Terms 8.1, agreement 10.3). The conditions of the offered models:

  • Kimi K3: a model-as-a-service business whose group revenue exceeds US$20 million in any 12 months needs a separate agreement with Moonshot AI, and a commercial product with more than 100 million monthly active users or US$20 million in monthly revenue must display "Kimi K3" in its interface.
  • GLM 5.3: a model-as-a-service business whose group revenue exceeds US$10 billion in any 12 months must pass Z.AI's security review before commercial use.
  • Qwen 3.8 Max: a model-as-a-service or AI work-assistant business whose group revenue exceeds US$50 million in any 12 months needs a separate license from Qwen, and a commercial product with more than 100 million monthly active users or US$20 million in monthly revenue must display the model's name.
  • MIT (GLM 5.3 Flash, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, MiMo V2.6, Ling 3.0) and Apache 2.0 (Gemma 4, Muse Glimmer 30B, Hunyuan Hy4 Preview, Qwen 3.8 27B): no field-of-use or revenue condition.
  • OpenMDW 1.1 (Laguna S 2.1): no condition on use or outputs; the license ends for anyone who sues claiming the model infringes a patent or copyright.

The Kimi K3, GLM-5.3 and Qwen 3.8 Max licenses also require every use to comply with applicable law. Every license requires the publisher's notices to stay with any copy of the weights; customers use the models on our fleets and receive no copy.

Limits of today's builds

  • Batches: Kimi K3 and GLM 5.3 serve at most 8 requests at once, GLM 5.3 Flash 16.
  • Speculation runs on Kimi K3 and GLM 5.3, for one request at a time. It speeds up text the request already contains, such as whole-file code rewrites (Kimi K3 15 to 126 tok/s, GLM 5.3 28 to 124 tok/s); on new prose the gain is 1.0 to 1.2 times.
  • Long prompts are slow from cold: prefill reaches 1 to 4% of the fleet's compute floor, so a 137,700-token prompt takes 299 s on Kimi K3. A follow-up turn that reuses the cached prefix takes about 5 s.
  • One model at a time holds a fleet. Loading another takes about a minute, up to 79 s for Kimi K3. A load inside a reserved hour counts as part of that hour (Hourly Terms 5.3).
