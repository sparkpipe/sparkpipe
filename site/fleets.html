Measured on one M5 Ultra Mac Studio, the Fleet 2 machine, with SparkPipe's Metal engine (2026-10-26): Qwen 3.8 27B at its full BF16 precision decodes 20.1 tok/s for one request, 90% of the 22.3 tok/s that the Studio's measured 1.14 TB/s of memory bandwidth allows. Several requests share each read of the weights: 39.0 tok/s in total for 2 requests, 130 for 8, 231 for 16, 366 for 32 and 442 for 64. Its prefill runs at 1,429 tok/s for a 1K-token prompt and 1,501 tok/s for 8K.

A 1M-token Kimi K3 prompt from cold is estimated at about 17 minutes with four accelerator boxes and 9 minutes with eight. Fleet 1 and Fleet 2 together hold 4 TB, and Fleet 3 adds 2 TB more, so a model too large for one fleet at its published precision can run across two: Qwen 3.8 Max in FP8 (2.5 TB) and Inkling in BF16 (1.9 TB).

How the estimates are made:

  • Decode: a token takes the time each node needs to read its share of the weights the token uses, at the 245 GB/s measured on Fleet 1, plus a fixed 0.44 ms per layer for the kernels and collectives around those reads. Kimi K3 measures 0.44 ms and GLM 5.3 0.31 ms; that fixed cost, not memory, is most of today's decode time, so the estimates rise as our work lowers it. Fleet 2 is taken at 90% of the Mac Studios' 1.2 TB/s with the same cost per layer and the model split over all eight, which are joined by 50 Gb/s links plus 10 GbE. Qwen 3.8 Max's 10.4 tok/s is not yet explained by this, so it has no Fleet 2 estimate. K-EXAONE 2.0's Fleet 2 estimate is being redone now that its experts are split evenly over the nodes.
  • Speculation on agent coding turns is taken as 1.5 times plain decode, as measured on short edits; whole-file rewrites measure 8.3 times and new prose 1.0 to 1.2 times.
  • Prefill accelerators are boxes of four RTX 5080 cards that reduce over PCIe inside the box and reach the fleets at 100 Gb/s each over a 400 Gb/s switch. Each long prompt streams the model's weights through the boxes once, at 85% of the links (42.5 GB/s with four boxes, 85 GB/s with eight), and the cards compute at 40% of their dense peak (about 450 TFLOPS each in FP8, 225 in BF16), including the attention cost of the prompt. Prompts shorter than about 20,000 tokens stay on the fleet's own prefill.
  • Qwen 3.8 Max at its published FP8 precision (2.5 TB) is larger than one fleet's 2 TB. Its row assumes one of its 4-bit builds (AMD MXFP4, Red Hat or RadixArk NVFP4), which fit one fleet and pass our accuracy A/B.

Model licenses

Each model is the publisher's open-weight software under its own license, which the customer must follow (Hourly Terms 8.1, agreement 10.3). The conditions of the offered models:

  • Kimi K3: a model-as-a-service business whose group revenue exceeds US$20 million in any 12 months needs a separate agreement with Moonshot AI, and a commercial product with more than 100 million monthly active users or US$20 million in monthly revenue must display "Kimi K3" in its interface.
  • GLM 5.3: a model-as-a-service business whose group revenue exceeds US$10 billion in any 12 months must pass Z.AI's security review before commercial use.
  • Qwen 3.8 Max: a model-as-a-service or AI work-assistant business whose group revenue exceeds US$50 million in any 12 months needs a separate license from Qwen, and a commercial product with more than 100 million monthly active users or US$20 million in monthly revenue must display the model's name.
  • MIT (GLM 5.3 Flash, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, MiMo V2.6, Ling 3.0) and Apache 2.0 (Gemma 4, Muse Glimmer 30B, Hunyuan Hy4 Preview, Qwen 3.8 27B, K-EXAONE 2.0, A.X K2): no field-of-use or revenue condition.
  • Upstage Solar License (Solar Open 2): the Apache 2.0 terms, plus a rule that a model derived from Solar Open 2 and distributed to others must state the "Solar" brand; no field-of-use or revenue condition.
  • OpenMDW 1.1 (Laguna S 2.1): no condition on use or outputs; the license ends for anyone who sues claiming the model infringes a patent or copyright.

The Kimi K3, GLM-5.3 and Qwen 3.8 Max licenses also require every use to comply with applicable law. Every license requires the publisher's notices to stay with any copy of the weights; customers use the models on our fleets and receive no copy.
