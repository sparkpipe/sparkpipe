Fleet List

Updated 1 November 2026

This page is the Fleet List, with the available models, that the SparkPipe Customer Agreement refers to. Every fleet is made up of machines we operate ourselves in Seoul. A fleet's Rate is the number of hour-credits (HC) one hour of it costs; a week on it costs 144 HC times its Rate. Fleets come in pairs that share a 32-port 100 Gb/s switch. Each pair's A fleet is pinned by the week. Its B fleet is an Hour Fleet, booked by the hour, and the A fleet's holder may also pin it for the weeks they hold the A fleet, so the pair runs as one cluster of 32 Sparks with 4 TB of memory. At least a quarter of the serving fleets always stay open for hours: 2 of 8 when all are serving. Any other fleet that nobody holds can also be booked by the hour, up to the end of the current week. Each account may hold up to 24 hours a week on Hour Fleets. Every fleet gives the customer who holds it 8 TB of cache and 4 TB of storage. The Korean version (fleets-ko.html) has the same content and prevails if they differ.

The terms are published for review. Hours on Hour Fleets can be booked from 1 December 2026, and weeks on a pinned fleet from 4 January 2027.

Each node keeps 1 TB of cache and 500 GB of customer storage on its own NVMe drive, mirrored to its pair partner over the direct pair link, so a fleet holds 8 TB of cache and 4 TB of storage, and a failed node loses neither. If both nodes of a pair fail together, their part of the cache has to be rebuilt, and the Customer Agreement credits 24 hours of the fleet for each such event (clause 6.6). Customer storage is working space: back up daily anything you want to keep.

Our system publishes live capacity, with each fleet's state, whether it is an Hour Fleet, pinned or available, and how many waitlist locks are waiting, at https://sparkpipe.ai/availability.txt, regenerated at least every 30 seconds.

Models on Fleet 1

Every model installed on Fleet 1. An offered model can be called on any fleet you hold, through our API with your own keys. A model a customer adds to its own storage is listed only in that customer's account. A fleet keeps as many models loaded at once as fit in its memory. A request for a model that is not loaded waits while it loads, usually 20 to 80 seconds. Loading it unloads another model only if they do not all fit, and the unloaded model's cache is kept for when it comes back. A core model gets ongoing performance work; a qualified model passed the release qualification and serves at the speeds shown; a verifying model serves and passes the chat checks while its tokens are compared with the publisher's reference implementation.

Choosing a precision (coming)

Every model will be offered at its publisher's full precision, the default, and also at 8 bits (FP8) and 4 bits (NVFP4 or MXFP4), and in EXL3 where good builds exist. You choose by model name, for example glm-5.3, glm-5.3-fp8 or glm-5.3-nvfp4. A lower precision runs faster and leaves more memory for the cache, so longer conversations or more requests fit at once. For every variant this list will show its speed, its accuracy against the full-precision model and its cache capacity, all measured, and each variant passes the same accuracy check before it is offered. How close to the hardware limit

Each step is bounded by the resource that saturates first. Decoding one request is bounded by memory: each of the 16 Sparks reads its sixteenth of the weights a token needs, at 273 GB/s. Prefill on 16 Sparks is bounded by the network: every layer adds up its partial results across all 16, about 10 GB/s a node in practice. Compute is 20 to 100 times further away. Long prompts (measured)

Measured on Fleet 1 alone, set to prefill only so that it serves no decode meanwhile: a 990,881-token Kimi K3 prompt prefills from cold in 16.2 minutes (971.6 s), with its first token 975.7 s and its answer 16.3 minutes (979.1 s) after the request; a 258,339-token prompt prefills in one 144.8 s pass, with its answer 150.0 s after the request. On a fleet you hold, a prompt is prefilled once and its cache stays, so later turns start from it.

Two fleets together hold 4 TB of memory, so a model too large for one fleet at its published precision can run across two, such as Qwen 3.8 Max in FP8 (2.5 TB) or Inkling in BF16 (1.9 TB) with room for its cache.

Model licenses

Each model is the publisher's open-weight software under its own license, which the customer must follow (Customer Agreement). The conditions of the offered models:

  • Kimi K3: a model-as-a-service business whose group revenue exceeds US$20 million in any 12 months needs a separate agreement with Moonshot AI, and a commercial product with more than 100 million monthly active users or US$20 million in monthly revenue must display "Kimi K3" in its interface.
  • GLM 5.3: a model-as-a-service business whose group revenue exceeds US$10 billion in any 12 months must pass Z.AI's security review before commercial use.
  • Qwen 3.8 Max: a model-as-a-service or AI work-assistant business whose group revenue exceeds US$50 million in any 12 months needs a separate license from Qwen, and a commercial product with more than 100 million monthly active users or US$20 million in monthly revenue must display the model's name.
  • MIT (GLM 5.3 Flash, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, MiMo V2.6, Ling 3.0, Motif 3) and Apache 2.0 (Gemma 4, Muse Glimmer 30B, Hunyuan Hy4 Preview, Qwen 3.8 27B, K-EXAONE 2.0, A.X K2): no field-of-use or revenue condition.
  • Upstage Solar License (Solar Open 2): the Apache 2.0 terms, plus a rule that a model derived from Solar Open 2 and distributed to others must state the "Solar" brand; no field-of-use or revenue condition.
  • OpenMDW 1.1 (Laguna S 2.1): no condition on use or outputs; the license ends for anyone who sues claiming the model infringes a patent or copyright.

The Kimi K3, GLM-5.3 and Qwen 3.8 Max licenses also require every use to comply with applicable law. Every license requires the publisher's notices to stay with any copy of the weights; customers use the models on our fleets and receive no copy.
