## 1. K1 — the keepalive that survives (the 3-death pattern ends here)

`tools/k3_keepalive.sh` (committed e88bb08) deployed as
`/home/sparke/k3build/keepalive.sh` (md5-identical to the committed file,
checked). Design, per the brief:

- runs `k3_pack.py` as a spawned child with the pid captured AT SPAWN;
  that pid is the ONLY pid it ever signals; TERM only, never KILL; no
  pkill/pgrep anywhere in the script;
- restarts the packer ONLY when the captured pid is confirmed gone (the
  journal makes every restart a RESUME, not a rebuild — 174.5 GB of
  already-written payload was the reason the old chain was worth
  babysitting; the journal-resume is why restarts are now cheap);
- a live-but-stalled packer is REPORTED (journal age + /proc state),
  never restarted — two writers on one pack = corruption;
- restart gates: the ceph warm source must serve 8 MB direct reads from
  four probe shards, and the node must have ≥20 GiB available;
- SIGTERM to the supervisor is relayed as TERM to the child; every event
  is logged with a timestamp to `/home/sparke/k3build/keepalive.log`.

Armed 21:42:14 for the stage-2 build (`keepalive.sh 48 23
/home/sparke/k3build/k3_stage_48_23.pack` under nohup, supervisor pid
logged). **The stage-2 build then ran to completion without a single
restart — the honest reading is that the loop was never exercised by an
actual death this session, but the probe gates DID engage on every poll
cycle and the supervisor is the same code path that would resume.** The
old `probe_and_resume.sh` is retired: it had a bash syntax error at line
12 (`done` — the bug that turned "re-arm" into a no-op during at least
one coordinator re-arm) and it exec'd the packer instead of supervising
