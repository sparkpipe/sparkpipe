## What landed

### C5 — reuse-value park policy (docs/JIT_KV_RESPONSE.md C5)

The victim choice behind every pager park-out is now a selectable,
admission-time policy knob. `SparkKvPagerConfiguration.park_policy` (the
configuration's former `reserved2` slot; validated at Initialize, anything
above REUSE_VALUE refused) installs the policy on its arena next to the
evict binding it already owns:

- `SPARK_KV_PAGER_PARK_POLICY_LRU` (0) — the default. The selectors' LRU
  branch is the historical comparison, byte for byte: existing suites
  (slice, wire, c3c4, nvme_tier, kv_cache) run green untouched.
- `SPARK_KV_PAGER_PARK_POLICY_REUSE_VALUE` (1) — keepness =
  `restore_count * 2^40 − age + dirty * 2^20` (int64, inputs clamped so the
  arithmetic is exact; weights orders of magnitude apart by construction):
  restored-again history dominates, dirtiness outranks recency, recency is
  the residual term. One definition lives in cache/kv_cache.c
  (`SparkKvCacheReuseValueKeepness`) and both resident-eviction selectors
  (the slot-scanning one the pager's admission/restore trims use, and the
  block-scan one behind `EvictResidentBlocksToLimit`) rank candidates
  through the same comparator; reference counts stay the primary key
  exactly as LRU had them.

The restored-again history is real block state: `SparkKvCacheBlock`
`reserved0` became `restore_count` (same struct size, ABI 6 undisturbed),
bumped by `SparkKvCacheArenaMarkParkedBlockResident` on each fresh
re-attachment — a completed page-in IS the restored-again event — and
cleared on recycle.

Fixtures (tests/test_jit_kv_c5w2.c, deterministic, single-threaded):

- A twice-restored block that LRU evicts (it is the LRU-oldest) SURVIVES
  under reuse-value; the once-restored newer block parks instead. The
  victim choice lands in both residency and the page-out receipt.
- A clean cold block drops before a dirty warm one under BOTH policies
  (the policies agree where recency and dirtiness point the same way).
- Dirtiness FLIPS an LRU decision: a dirty older block survives and a
  clean younger one parks only under reuse-value (parking the dirty block
  would cost a write; endurance is the line item).
- Budget-accounting invariance: identical op sequences under the two
  policies hold identical page-out/in counts and bytes, admission
  counters, park completions/failures, resident/reserved/unassigned, tier
  slots, and the device-law receipt — the page-out receipt's final entry
  (0 vs 1) is the ONLY recorded difference, which is the point.

### W2 — nvme_tier's deadline lookahead as the C2 gate's engine

The dispatch offer now carries an optional deadline hint
(`SparkKvPagerDispatch.deadline_step`, the struct's former `reserved0`;
0 = no hint, so historically-constructed offers behave exactly as before):

- Pager: `SparkKvPagerRestoreBlockDeadline` (new; the 3-arg
  `SparkKvPagerRestoreBlock` is its hintless wrapper) forwards the hint on
  EVERY RequestDemand poll of the gated restore.
  `SparkKvPagerDispatchBlock` feeds the offer's hint into it.
- Tier: `SparkNvmeTierRequestDemandDeadline` is the read path; the legacy
  `SparkNvmeTierRequestDemand` is its hintless wrapper. With a hint, a
  PRESENT record the tier cannot take yet (saturation) is ORDERED in the
  pending debt — a queued entry tightens to the deadline (the pending heap
  is earliest-deadline-first, so the gated block becomes the next read
  issued), an unqueued one enqueues at the deadline — and answers BUSY
  with `result.ordered = 1` (the demand result's former `reserved0`).
  With staging free the hint issues immediately, exactly what the legacy
  path would have taken. The hintless path keeps the pre-W2 semantics
  byte for byte: yank-before-acquire, spin, `demand_stalls` accounting.
- Pager restore loop: an ORDERED busy answers after ONE pump —
  `SPARK_STATUS_BUSY` → the C2 gate translates to QUEUED (the re-offer IS
  the queue). Burning the 64-poll budget spinning on a saturated tier was
  the wedge-shaped hole; the hintless path keeps the historical spin.
- Observability: `SparkNvmeTierStatistics.demand_deadline_orders` counts
  gated blocks pulled forward in the debt queue.

Fixture (one saturated scenario, two bit-identical runs — four parked
blocks, the lookahead queueing all of them at one far deadline, two
backlog reads in flight on a drive whose cancellations never land, so the
staging is saturated and unstealable):

- HINTED (deadline = now): the gate answers QUEUED (healthy backpressure,
  no error), the tier's pump inside the offer drains the debt queue head —
  the gated block — and its read completes BEFORE the FIFO-older peer's
  (device completion ordinals prove the order).
- BASELINE (no hint): the gate yanks the block out of the debt queue,
  spins its poll budget on the saturated tier, and answers a hard
  IO_ERROR; the block lost its place and completes AFTER the peer. Still
  no wedge — the block restores on a later offer — but the order is
  inverted and the gate's failure is loud instead of queued.

## Honesty notes

- The first run of the c5w2 fixture caught a REAL bug in this lane's own
  counter: the tighten check re-read the heap slot AFTER the sift-up had
  moved a different entry into it, so `demand_deadline_orders` under-
  counted (the EDF behavior itself was correct). Fixed by deciding the
  increment from the pre-tighten value.
- The W2 baseline (hintless) dispatch answers IO_ERROR under this
  saturation — that is the pre-existing behavior this lane keeps on the
  hintless path (byte-for-byte) and the fixture asserts it as the
  pathology the hint removes. Changing the hintless spin was explicitly
  out of scope; if the program wants the queue-not-wedge answer on the
  hintless path too, that is a one-line follow-up for the integration
  reviewer to schedule.
- `request.staging` acquire under a hint still counts one demand_stall
  inside `NvmeTierStagingAcquireForDemand` before the order is taken —
  the stall is the honest "no buffer this instant"; the ORDER is the new
  part. Not counted twice on the pager side.
