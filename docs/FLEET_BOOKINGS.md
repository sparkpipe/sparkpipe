# Fleet bookings: hour-credits, Hours, Weeks and the waitlist

This is the design of the payment and booking service for the elite-developer offer. The SparkPipe Customer Agreement (`site/terms/agreement-*.md`) governs it, and this document follows its clause numbers; where they differ, the Agreement wins. The published settings live in `site/pricing.js`. The code is `tools/sparkpipe_market.py` (bookings, the waitlist, auto-extend, keys, the gateway's checks, notices, the rebuild and downtime credits, the status page and maintenance) and `tools/sparkpipe_pay.py` (accounts, sessions, orders, payment processors, the ledger, the gateway to the hub, uptime records, usage records, webhook delivery and the HTTP service). The customer-facing API is `docs/FLEET_API.md`; this document is how the VPS side of it works. Payments are described in `docs/PAYMENTS.md`.

## What a customer buys

- **Hour-credits (HC).** HC is the only currency. It is bought in any quantity from `hc.min`, to six decimal places (HC is counted to six decimals, clause 13.2), with no maximum.
  - **Price per HC** depends on the quantity in one purchase: the `hc.prices` entry with the largest `from` at most the quantity sets the price, in won (`krw`) for Toss Payments and in US dollars (`usd`) for stablecoins and USD bank transfers (SWIFT). Today 143 HC cost $2,860 or ₩3,889,600 and 144 HC cost $2,160 or ₩2,937,600, before VAT: 10% (`vatRate`), or 0% (`swiftVatRate`) on a USD bank transfer (SWIFT) under clause 3.3(b) (`docs/PAYMENTS.md`).
  - **Lots.** Each purchase, sandbox purchase and credit is a lot that lapses `hc.validityMonths` (12) months after it is issued, on the same day of the month and at the same time (a lot issued on 29 February lapses on 28 February).
  - **Spending.** HC is spent when a booking is made, from the lot that lapses first. Spent HC has left its lot, so it never lapses, however far ahead the booking runs (clause 13.3). Unspent HC in a lot lapses with it.
- **Hours** (clause 14). Whole hours on one Fleet, starting on the hour, not yet started and not before `hoursFrom` (00:00 KST, outside the sandbox; refusal `not_open`) on any Fleet, each costing `bookings.hourHC` × Rate × (1 − the account's discount when it books).
  - **The account's discount** is the larger of: the `bookings.discounts` entry for N, where N is the account's booked Weeks that have not ended, the current Week included, on the Fleet where it holds the most (Weeks on different Fleets are not added); and, while it is on the waitlist, the discount of the Weeks its lock would book at the Sunday round on a Fleet at Rate 1 with no Weeks held (below). The quote, the booking line (`discount`) and the account view (`discount`: `discount`, `weeks`, `basis` `held` or `waitlist` or null, `fleet` for `held`) show it. A booking keeps the discount it was made at.
  - **Leaving repays the waitlist part** (clause 16.1). An Hour priced at the waitlist discount records `waitlist_saving` on its booking line: the HC the waitlist discount saved beyond the held discount. When the account leaves the waitlist (`/market/waitlist/leave`), the savings of its Hours booked since it joined are debited from the HC the lock releases (`hc_debit`, reason `waitlist_discount`, `ref` the bookings), and the answer says `repaid`. A lock that the Sunday round turns into Weeks, or that a lapse unlocks, repays nothing. The account view shows the amount due on leaving as `waitlistRepay`; the quote shows `waitlist_saving`. So a lock taken only for the discount and dropped before the Sunday round gains nothing.
  - **On an Hour Fleet** (`"hourFleet": true` on the Fleet List; there may be several): ending at most `bookings.hourFleetDaysAhead` days from now. Each account may hold at most `bookings.hourFleetHoursPerWeek` Hours on all Hour Fleets together in each Week; an Hour booking that crosses midnight between Sunday and Monday counts in both Weeks. Weeks of a pinned pair (below) are not Hours and do not count. An Hour Fleet is pinned only as a pair and never allocated to the waitlist, so HC always has Fleets to be spent on.
  - **On any other serving Fleet** that is not pinned and that nobody holds in those hours, ending at most at the end of the current Week (Sunday 24:00 KST), so an Hour never blocks a Monday handover. A Fleet pinned in the current Week takes no Hours, from anyone, its holder included.
  - No Hour overlaps announced maintenance.
- **Weeks** (clause 15). Monday 00:00 to Sunday 24:00 KST (Sunday 15:00 UTC to Sunday 15:00 UTC), on a serving Fleet that is not an Hour Fleet. A booked Week pins the Fleet: nobody else books any of its hours or Weeks.
  - **Price:** `bookings.weekHC` × Rate × (1 − discount) for each Week booked. The discount is the largest `bookings.discounts[].off` whose `weeks` is at most N, where N is the account's booked Weeks on that Fleet that have not ended, the current Week included, plus the Weeks being booked. It applies only to the Weeks in this booking. With today's settings: 3 Weeks cost 432 HC, 4 cost 547.2 (5% off), 13 cost 1,684.8 (10%), 26 cost 3,182.4 (15%) and 52 cost 5,990.4 (20%).
  - **First Week:** an account that holds no Week of the Fleet that has not ended books from the next Monday 00:00 KST, on a Fleet free for that Week, and only while no waitlist lock could pay for a Week of that Fleet (refusal `waitlist`): the waitlist comes first and gets free Fleets at the Sunday allocation. No Week starts before `weeksFrom` (outside the sandbox).
  - **Extension:** an account that holds Weeks of the Fleet books the Weeks right after its last booked Week; there is no other choice of start, so extensions are contiguous. The last Week may start at most `bookings.maxWeeksAhead` (52) Weeks after the current Week, and one booking holds 1 to 52 Weeks.
  - **Keeping the Fleet:** the holder's deadline is 00:00 KST on the Sunday of its last booked Week. Before it, nobody else may book the following Week, nor any Hour in it (refusal `pinned`). At the deadline the Sunday round (below) may allocate the Fleet to the waitlist; if it does not, the holder (as an extension) or anyone else (as a first Week, which is then the next Monday) may book that Week until it starts.
- **Pairs** (clause 15.8). The Fleet List pairs Fleets (`pair` on both entries); in each pair the A Fleet is pinned by the Week and the B Fleet is an Hour Fleet. While an account holds Weeks of the A Fleet, it, and only it, may pin the B Fleet through `/market/quote-weeks` and `/market/book-weeks` with the B Fleet's id:
  - **Which Weeks:** a first pair Week is the next Monday, or the first later Week with no Hours booked on the B Fleet; extensions follow the last pair Week. Every pair Week must be a Week the account holds of the A Fleet (refusal `pair_not_held` otherwise, and for anyone else).
  - **Price:** `weekHC` × the B Fleet's Rate × (1 − the discount for N), where N is the account's Weeks of the A Fleet that have not ended, the current Week included. The quote says `pair`.
  - **Open Hour Fleets:** in every Week at least `bookings.openHourShare` (0.25, the Agreement's floor) of the serving Fleets, rounded up, stay open as unpinned Hour Fleets: 2 of 8 at launch, 1 of 4 at 64 Sparks. A pair pin that would leave fewer in any of its Weeks is refused (`pair_limit`). The public state's `pairs` gives `open` (pins still possible for the next Week) and `limit`.
  - **Keeping it:** a pinned B Fleet takes no Hours, and before its holder's Sunday 00:00 deadline nobody else books an Hour in the Week after its last pinned Week (refusal `pinned`), so the holder, or auto-extend while it waits on the terms or the balance, can still book that Week. It is kept by booking its next Week like any pinned Fleet, and auto-extend books it right after the A Fleet (the A Fleet's id sorts first). If the A Fleet's next Week is not booked yet, the B Fleet's extension waits; when the B Fleet's Weeks end without it, auto-extend stops with reason `pair_not_held`. The Sunday round never allocates an Hour Fleet.
- **Waitlist** (clause 16).
  - **Joining:** lock at least `bookings.weekHC` HC of the unlocked balance; afterwards any amount may be added. Locked HC is taken out of its lots, recorded by lot, and cannot be spent on bookings. Leaving unlocks all of it back into its lots, then repays the waitlist discount of the Hours booked since joining (above).
  - **Operator email:** each lock created or increased emails `SPARKPIPE_PAY_NOTIFY` (subject `Waitlist lock: <company>, <lock> HC (+<change>)`) with the account, the new lock, the change, the position and the number of locks. The mail is queued after the lock is written, so a mail that cannot be sent is only logged and never fails the lock.
  - **Order:** largest lock first, then earliest join (the ledger sequence of the first `lock` line). Adding keeps the join time. A customer sees only its own lock and position. The public state and `availability.txt` show only how many accounts hold a lock (`waitlist.locks`): no positions, amounts or identities.
  - **The Sunday round:** at each Sunday 00:00 KST, each serving Fleet that is not an Hour Fleet, in Fleet List order, that has no booking in the following Week goes to the first customer in order whose lock pays for at least one Week on it; that customer leaves the order. Its lock books the largest number of consecutive Weeks from the next Monday, up to `maxWeeksAhead`, whose price under clause 15.2 is at most the lock, and the rest of the lock is unlocked. The price is not monotone in the count (25 Weeks at 10% off cost 3,240 HC, 26 at 15% cost 3,182.4), so a lock of 3,200 HC books 26 Weeks. The customer is emailed.
  - **Lapse while locked:** when a lot lapses, the HC it has locked is removed from the lock and recorded as a refund due (`lock_lapse`): for a purchased lot, its share of the order's total with VAT, in the order's currency, through the order's processor; credited lots carry no refund. `SPARKPIPE_PAY_NOTIFY` is emailed the list. A lock that falls below `bookings.weekHC` leaves the waitlist and the rest is unlocked.
- **Auto-extend** (`docs/FLEET_API.md` §16). Missing a Sunday deadline costs a holder the Fleet and its working state, so auto-extend is on by default.
  - **On from the first Week:** the first Week an account books of a Fleet (one it holds no Week of that has not ended), and every Week the waitlist grants it, turns auto-extend on for that Fleet with one Week ahead: an `auto_extend` line with `weeks: 1` and `default: true`, unless the account already has a setting for that Fleet. Extensions never turn it back on.
  - **Changing it:** `PUT /market/auto-extend {"fleet", "weeks_ahead"}`, with a session and the current terms, keeps `weeks_ahead` (1 to `maxWeeksAhead`) Weeks booked after the current Week on a Fleet the account holds Weeks of (409 `not_pinned` otherwise); `weeks_ahead: 0` turns it off. The account page shows the setting under Fleets for each pinned Fleet, with a one-click switch to turn it off (or on) and a choice of Weeks ahead (`site/market.js`).
  - **Booking:** the setting books at once what is missing, and then, at each Monday 00:00 KST when the count drops, one more Week as an extension, at the discount for the Weeks held, from the account's unlocked HC. The extension is due on the Monday of the last booked Week at the latest, so it always comes before the holder's Sunday deadline. With the default of one Week ahead, a holder who does nothing has the next Week booked at 00:00 KST on the Monday of its last booked Week. Each booking is a `booking` line with `auto: true` and sends `booking.confirmed`, whose text says whether auto-extend is on.
  - **Terms and balance:** a due extension waits, without booking, while the account has not accepted the current Customer Agreement (clause 9.1: no Booking until it accepts) or its unlocked HC does not cover the price. It writes an `auto_extend_wait` line and sends `booking.deadline` at once, early, with `early: true`, `reason` (`terms` or `insufficient_hc`), the `price` and the `balance`, and `hc_needed` for a short balance, once for each reason for that Week. It books as soon as the account accepts and the balance covers it. The reminders before the deadline (below) still follow.
  - **Stopping:** if the pinned Weeks run out unextended, or the Fleet is taken or stops serving, auto-extend stops (`auto_extend` with `weeks: 0` and a `reason`) and sends `booking.deadline` with `stopped: true`, the `reason`, `through` and `deadline`. It is a notice like the others, so webhooks subscribed to `booking.deadline` receive it, and it is always emailed, whatever the account's email subscription.
- **Downtime credit** (clause 4.4): automatic, from the uptime records (below).
- **Maintenance** (clause 4.3): `sparkpipe_pay.py maintenance FLEET MONDAY [HOURS]` schedules up to `bookings.maintenanceHoursPerWeek` hours per Fleet per Week, starting at least 7 days ahead, on the consecutive hours with the fewest booked hours, then the KST start hour least used by that Fleet's earlier maintenance (so the times rotate), then the earliest. Holders it overlaps are emailed at once. When it ends, every booked hour it overlaps is credited as a lot of HC equal to the booking's Rate (clause 4.4) and a `maintenance_done` line is written.

## Events and their order

Time-driven events run whenever the service touches the market (every request and a 30-second tick), in time order, and at the same instant lapses first, then the round, then maintenance credits, then downtime credits, then auto-extend, then deadline notices:

1. a lot that lapses while some of it is locked;
2. the Sunday round, for each Sunday 00:00 KST after the later of the last recorded round and the earliest join on the waitlist (there is no round while the waitlist is empty);
3. the end of a maintenance period;
4. a downtime credit that is due (Uptime and the downtime credit, below);
5. an auto-extension that is due;
6. `booking.deadline` 72, 48, 6 and 2 hours before 00:00 KST on the Sunday of an account's last booked Week of a Fleet (`DEADLINE_HOURS`). Each is sent once (the `once` key of its notice), with `hours`, `deadline`, `through`, the `balance`, the price of the next Week (`next_week_hc`), `auto_extend` (the Weeks ahead, 0 when off) and, when auto-extend waits, `waiting` (`terms` or `insufficient_hc`); its text says how to turn auto-extend on, or why it waits. After an outage only the latest mark that has passed is sent, and none once the deadline has passed. Extending moves the deadline, so the next notices follow the new last Week, and a holder whose auto-extend books on Monday gets none.

Each event is written with the instant it happened (`now`), so a service that was down, or a sandbox whose clock jumped, catches up with the same result.

## API

Everything is under `/pay/api`, and the sandbox is under `/pay/sandbox/api`. Times are UTC strings: `stamp` (`2027-01-06T03:00:00Z`) for instants and `slot_text` (`2027-01-10T15:00Z`) for hours. HC amounts are strings with six decimals. A session is the page's cookie or, for the CLI, `Authorization: Bearer sps-…` from the email-code sign-in (`docs/PAYMENTS.md`). Refusals are `{"error", "reason"}` with the HTTP status; the reasons a page acts on are `terms` (409: accept the current Agreement and send `accept: true`), `insufficient_hc` (402), `pinned`, `taken`, `too_far`, `not_open`, `started`, `hour_cap`, `not_weekly`, `pair_not_held`, `pair_limit`, `maintenance`, `lock_too_small`, `quote_changed` (409).

## The gateway

The gateway passes every path of `docs/FLEET_API.md` §2 that the fleet serves through to the hub's chat layer of that Fleet over its tunnel: `SPARKPIPE_FLEET_UPSTREAMS` maps each Fleet to its hub (`F1B=http://127.0.0.1:18081/v1 F1A=http://127.0.0.1:18083/v1`), and a Fleet it does not list uses `SPARKPIPE_POOL_UPSTREAM` (`http://127.0.0.1:18081/v1`). Paths other than `/v1/...` go to the same host without `/v1`. Paths outside that list, such as the hub's own `/v1/sparkpipe/...` controls, and any path with a `.` or `..` segment, even percent-encoded, answer 404 and never reach the hub.

- **Credentials.** `Authorization: Bearer sk-hc-…`, `X-Api-Key: sk-hc-…` (what Claude Code sends with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`), or Basic authentication with the key as the password (what git sends; a `/git/` request without one gets 401 with `WWW-Authenticate: Basic realm="SparkPipe"`). The account session (cookie or bearer) works too, with every scope and `allow_swap`; a cookie request from another `Origin` is refused.
- **Scopes.** Each path needs one scope of the key: `inference` for `/v1/messages`, `/v1/responses`, `/v1/chat/completions`, `/v1/models`, `/v1/codex/models`, `/api/hello`, `/v1/tokenize`, `/v1/detokenize`, `DELETE /v1/requests/{id}`, `/v1/batches` and `/mcp`; `state` for `/v1/state`, `/v1/cache`, `/v1/packs` and reading `/v1/models/resident`, `/v1/models/aliases` and `/v1/drafters`; `models` for changing those and for `/v1/custom-models`; `storage` for `/v1/repos`, `/git/`, `/v1/storage`, `/v1/export`, `/v1/restore` and `/v1/fetch`; `functions` for `/v1/functions`, `/v1/runs`, `/v1/schedules` and `/v1/reports`. The hub gets the key's scopes too, so the MCP server can refuse tools by scope.
- **Holder check on every path.** The Fleet is the key's `fleet` if it has one; otherwise `X-SparkPipe-Fleet`, or `sparkpipe.fleet` in a chat completions body; otherwise the one Fleet the account holds now. An account that holds no Fleet, or not the one named, gets 403 `fleet_not_held`; one holding several and naming none gets 409 `fleet_required`. `POST /v1/state/move` needs both `from` and `to` held, and goes to `from`.
- **Headers to the hub.** The gateway also sends `X-SparkPipe-Member` and `X-SparkPipe-Role` (Teams, below). The customer's headers pass unchanged, without hop-by-hop headers, `Host`, `Authorization`, `X-Api-Key`, `Cookie`, `X-Forwarded-*` and every `X-SparkPipe-*` header other than `X-SparkPipe-Priority` and `X-SparkPipe-Source-Token`. The gateway adds `Authorization: Bearer` the upstream key, `X-SparkPipe-Public: 1`, `X-SparkPipe-Account`, `X-SparkPipe-Fleet`, `X-SparkPipe-Key` (the key id, or `session`), `X-SparkPipe-Scopes` and `X-SparkPipe-Allow-Swap` (`1` or `0`), and `X-SparkPipe-Priority` from the key when the request sends none. A customer's own copies of these never reach the hub. The source token is passed on and never logged or stored.
- **Bodies** up to 32 MiB (413 above), streamed to the hub as they arrive. The holder check runs before any of the body is read; only `POST /v1/chat/completions` without a named Fleet and `POST /v1/state/move` read their body for the Fleet, after checking that the account holds one. A chunked body, which git sends for a large push, is read whole up to 32 MiB and sent with `Content-Length`; a chunk size that is not 1 to 8 hex digits, or a chunk cut short, answers 400. A chat completions body loses its `sparkpipe` object; every other body is sent byte for byte.
- **Answers** come back unchanged: status, headers other than hop-by-hop ones, and the body, streamed as the hub sends it, so SSE streams, keep-alives and the hub's own errors (such as 409 `model_not_loaded`) pass through. A hub that does not answer gives 502 `upstream_error`. When the caller disconnects, the connection to the hub closes, so the hub can stop generation.
- **WebSocket.** `GET /v1/responses` with `Upgrade: websocket` is passed to the hub with the same headers; after the hub's 101 the gateway relays bytes both ways. Apache passes the upgrade with `upgrade=websocket` on the `/pay/api/` proxy (`deployment/vps/sparkpipe-pay.conf`).
- **End of the hold.** Every connection to the hub, HTTP or WebSocket, is watched. Every `TUNNEL_CHECK` (30) seconds the gateway checks the credential again (a revoked, expired or retired key, a key used from an address now outside its `ips`, or an ended session closes it at once) and finds when the account's hold of the Fleet ends, counting back-to-back bookings as one hold. It closes the connection, both to the caller and to the hub, 30 seconds after the hold ends (clause 14.3).
- **Errors** of the gateway use the API shape's own body: Anthropic's `{"type": "error", "error": {"type", "message"}}` on `/v1/messages`, and `{"error": {"message", "type", "code"}}` elsewhere, with the codes of `docs/FLEET_API.md` §17 (`invalid_key`, `fleet_required`, `fleet_not_held`, `invalid_request`, `upstream_error`, `fleet_down` (503) when the held Fleet is down and no free Fleet can serve it, and `busy` with `Retry-After` when an account without an active rental sends more than 6,000 requests in ten minutes, or a holder more than its share of the gateway, `SPARKPIPE_GATEWAY_CAPACITY` divided by the Fleets rented now; `docs/FLEET_API.md` §17).
- **Failover.** A request for a held Fleet that is down goes to a free Fleet instead (Failover, below).
- **Sandbox.** The sandbox simulates `/v1/chat/completions` and `/v1/models` for a holder, and answers 503 `closed` on the other paths.

## Keys

`POST /v1/keys` or `POST /market/keys` creates a key with these options, all optional: `note` (one line, at most 100 characters), `scopes` (a list of `inference`, `state`, `storage`, `functions`, `models`, `account:read`; all of them by default), `fleet` (a Fleet id), `allow_swap` (false by default), `expires` (a UTC time in the future), `ips` (at most 32 addresses or networks, IPv4 or IPv6) and `priority` (`interactive` or `background`, the default for requests that send no `X-SparkPipe-Priority`). A bad option answers 400 `invalid_request`.

- **Checks.** A revoked or expired key, or a rotated one more than an hour after its rotation, answers 401 `invalid_key`. A key used from an address outside its `ips` (the address Apache adds last to `X-Forwarded-For`), or on a path outside its scopes, answers 403 `invalid_key`.
- **Rotation.** `POST /v1/keys/{id}/rotate` issues a new key with the same options and keeps the old one valid for one hour (`retires`). A key is rotated once.
- **Who manages keys.** Listing needs a session or a key with `account:read`; creating, revoking and rotating need a session. Each key belongs to the member who made it (`member`); the owner and admins list and manage every key of the account, and a member only its own (Teams, below).

## Usage records

The hub posts one record per request to `POST /hub/requests` (`{"records": [...]}`, 1 to 10,000 a call, or one record), with the upstream key. A record keeps only these fields, and everything else, prompts and replies included, is dropped: `request`, `account`, `fleet` and `at`, `member` (the team member, filled in from the key when the hub sends none) (a UTC time such as `2027-01-05T09:00:00Z`, from the last year), which are required, and `key`, `model`, `pack`, `api`, `priority`, `session`, `status`, `prompt_tokens`, `cached_tokens`, `prefill_tokens`, `queue_ms`, `prefill_ms`, `ttft_ms`, `output_tokens`, `duration_ms`, `decode_tps`, `draft_accept` and `cold_prefill_ms_estimate` (the telemetry of `docs/FLEET_API.md` §3.6). A record whose `request` is already stored for that day is a duplicate and is skipped, so the hub can resend. The answer is `accepted`, `duplicates` and `refused` (`index`, `error`).

Records are stored in `requests/<account>/<day>.jsonl` in the data directory and deleted 366 days after their day, so each is kept a year. When the customer asks in writing, they are deleted at once (DPA clause 9.3 promises within 30 days): `DELETE /v1/requests` with the owner's session, or `sparkpipe_pay.py erase-records EMAIL NOTE` for a request that arrives by email, deletes all of the account's records, writes a `records_erased` ledger line (`account`, `through`, `records`, `by`, `note`), adds `records.erased` to the audit log, emails the owner, and emails `SPARKPIPE_PAY_NOTIFY` the command that deletes the hub's copy within the same 30 days. A record the hub sends again for a time up to `through` is refused (`erased`), so deleted records never come back. The hub's own request records are deleted on the hub (`docs/LITELLM_FRONTEND.md`). `GET /v1/requests` reads only the account's own files for the days asked, without holding the lock that takes in new records, and keeps only one page in memory. It lists the account's own records in `[since, until)` (default the last 7 days, at most a year apart), optionally by `key`, `fleet`, `model` or `session`, `limit` (1 to 10,000, default 1,000) at a time with `after` set to the last answer's `next`. `totals` sums the whole window, for `sparkpipe stats`: `requests`, the token and time fields, `prefill_avoided_ms` (the sum of `cold_prefill_ms_estimate`), `cached_share` and `decode_tps` (output tokens over decode time).

## Notifications

Every event of `docs/FLEET_API.md` §13 is a notice, written to `notices/<day>.jsonl` when it happens and then delivered to the account's webhooks and email. A notice holds `notice`, `account`, `type`, `data`, `summary`, `text`, `targets` (webhook ids and `mail`) and an optional `once` key that keeps it from being sent twice. Notices are kept 30 days and then deleted, with the outcome of each delivery.

- **Where the events come from.** The market sends `booking.confirmed` (every booking, auto-extensions included), `booking.deadline` (above, early from auto-extend, and when auto-extend stops), `fleet.allocated` (the Sunday round), `waitlist.position` (whenever the account's own place changes because of someone else, a lapse or a round; never the other accounts' places) and `fleet.failover` (Failover, above), `downtime.credited` (a maintenance credit, the automatic downtime credit, and every operator credit under clause 4.4: `sparkpipe_pay.py credit EMAIL HC NOTE`, which tells the customer the `note`). The hub posts `run.finished`, `run.failed`, `report.ready`, `fetch.finished`, `fetch.failed`, `fetch.needs_token`, `pack.warm`, `cache.restored` and `models.changed` to `POST /hub/events` (`{"events": [{"id", "account", "fleet", "type", "data", "summary"}]}`, with the upstream key); an `id` already seen for that Fleet is a duplicate. `working_state.lost` comes with the rebuild credit (below).
- **Webhooks.** `PUT /v1/webhooks {"url": "https://…", "events": [...] or ["*"], "secret", "slack"}`, with the session, adds a webhook or replaces the one with the same URL; at most 10 an account. Without `secret` we make one and show it once. Webhooks, with their secrets, are kept in `webhooks.json` (mode 0600) until the account deletes them. The URL must be `https` with no user name, and must resolve only to public internet addresses other than our own hosts; the address is checked again and pinned at each delivery, and redirects are not followed. `DELETE /v1/webhooks/{id}` deletes it, its address and its secret.
- **Delivery.** A `POST` of `{"id", "type", "created", "account", "summary", "data"}`, or `{"text": summary}` when `slack` is true, with `X-SparkPipe-Event`, `X-SparkPipe-Delivery` (the notice id) and `X-SparkPipe-Signature: t=<unix time>,v1=<hex HMAC-SHA256 of "<t>." + body under the secret>`. A receiver checks the HMAC and that `t` is recent. Each delivery has 20 seconds in all (`WEBHOOK_SECONDS`), from connecting to the answer's status line; four workers deliver at once, so a slow receiver delays nobody else. Any answer other than 2xx, or none in time, is retried after 1 minute, 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 2, 6, 12 and 24 hours, and then given up. Each outcome is a line of `deliveries/<day>.jsonl`; on a restart, and every five minutes, deliveries of the last 3 days that have no outcome are sent again, notices written by an operator command included.
- **Email.** The notices the Agreement makes us send always go to the account's email, whatever it subscribes to: `fleet.allocated` (clause 16.3), `working_state.lost` (clause 6.6) and `downtime.credited` (clauses 4.4 and 6.4). Unless the account sets its own email subscription, `booking.deadline` and `fleet.failover` go there too. `PUT /v1/webhooks {"email": "<the account's email>", "events": [...]}` chooses the other events by email, and `events: []` turns them off; `GET /v1/webhooks` lists the three in `emailAlways`. The subscription is kept without the address, which is always the owner's.

## Teams (`docs/FLEET_API.md` §12)

An account's owner, the email that opened it, invites members by email. Each signs in with its own email code and acts for the account with its own role.

- **Roles.** `owner` (bookings, payments and members), `admin` (keys, Functions, models and storage) and `member` (its own keys, inference and its own Function runs). Only the owner books Hours and Weeks, buys HC, sets auto-extend, joins or leaves the waitlist and changes members (403 `role` otherwise). The owner and admins manage webhooks and read the audit log and the ledger export; payments (`orders` on `/account`, `GET /order/<id>`) are the owner's alone, so an admin's ledger export leaves them out (below).
- **Scopes by role.** The owner and admins have every scope; a member has `inference`, `functions` and `account:read`. A member's session reaches only those, a member makes keys only with those and without `allow_swap`, and every key is limited at each request to its member's current role, so moving an admin to member takes effect at once.
- **Invitations.** `POST /v1/members {"email", "role"}` with the owner's session invites `admin` or `member` (a `member_invite` line, valid 7 days) and emails the invitee; sent again for a member of the account, it changes the role (`member_role`). It refuses the owner's email, an email that is another account's or has another account's pending invitation (409 `email_taken`), and more than 50 members and invitations. The invitee joins by signing in with its email code (`POST /verify` with no company): a `member` line, with a `MB…` id, and the owner is emailed. An invitee that sends its own `company` and `accept: true` instead opens its own account and declines the invitation (a `member_remove` line with `declined`). An account may send 20 invitations a day and an email may receive 3; inviting a pending invitee again with the same role returns its invitation without another email.
- **Listing and removing.** `GET /v1/members` lists the owner, the members (`id`, `email`, `role`, `since`) and the pending invitations, with each role's scopes. `DELETE /v1/members/{id}` with the owner's session removes a member (`member_remove`, audited as `member.removed`), revokes its keys and ends its sessions, or cancels an invitation; a removed member's cookie no longer signs it in. A member may leave with its own id and its own session, whatever its role (audited as `member.left`).
- **Sessions.** A session belongs to a member: the cookie is signed for the member's id (the account's id for the owner), and `sessions.json` records every cookie and bearer session with its `member`. Signing out or `POST /market/sessions/revoke` ends either kind: a bearer session's record is deleted, and a cookie session's record is marked `revoked`, so that cookie no longer signs in (`docs/PAYMENTS.md`). `/account` and `/market/state` give the member's own `email`, `member` and `role`; `/market/state` lists the live sessions with `via` (`cookie` or `bearer`), a member its own, the owner every session.
- **To the hub.** Every forwarded request carries `X-SparkPipe-Member` (the account's id for the owner) and `X-SparkPipe-Role`, and `X-SparkPipe-Scopes` and `X-SparkPipe-Allow-Swap` reflect the role. The chat layer shares sequences between the members of an account by `X-SparkPipe-Member` (no member over three quarters of them while another waits) and keeps each member's replayed turns its own, whatever key it uses (`docs/LITELLM_FRONTEND.md`).
- **Usage per member.** Usage records carry `member`, from the hub or from the key; `GET /v1/requests?member=` filters by it, and a member sees only its own.
- **Audit log.** `audit/<account>/<day>.jsonl` (mode 600 in a 700 folder), kept a year like the usage records, holds an entry (`id`, `at`, `member`, `type`, `data`, without prompts or replies) for each sign-in and sign-out, each member, key, session and webhook change, each booking, waitlist lock and auto-extend setting, each order or sandbox purchase, and the system's own auto-extensions and allocations (`member: system`). The hub adds its own events, such as model switches, wipes, storage access, fetches and Function runs, with `POST /hub/audit {"events": [{"id", "account", "member", "fleet", "type", "at", "data"}]}` and the upstream key: a `type` of the form `models.switched`, `cache.wiped`, `storage.read`, `fetch.started` or `run.started` (prefixes `model`, `models`, `cache`, `storage`, `fetch`, `run`, `export`, `restore`, `pack`, `repo`), `data` of at most 4 KiB, once per `id` (stored as `HB<id>`). `GET /v1/audit` pages it like `/v1/requests`, by `type` and `member`.

## Rebuild credit (clause 6.6)

The hub posts each pair-loss report to `POST /hub/pair-loss` with the upstream key: `fleet`, `event` (the hub's id for the loss), `account`, optional `at` (default now), `pair` (the nodes), `contexts` and `storage` (what was lost, at most 1,000 each) and `run` (the rebuild run it started). If the account held the Fleet at `at`, the service credits at once a `rebuild` lot of 24 × the Rate of the booking that covered `at`, with `ref` `<fleet>:<event>`: one ledger line, which names the Fleet and the event and nothing that was lost. It then sends `working_state.lost` with what was lost, the credit and the run (a notice, kept 30 days), and emails `SPARKPIPE_PAY_NOTIFY`. The answer is `fleet`, `event`, `account`, `hc`, `lot`, `credited` and `duplicate`. The same `fleet` and `event` again, found by the lot's `ref`, answers the first credit with `duplicate: true` and credits nothing; if the service stopped after the credit and before the notice, that retry sends the notice. It answers 409 `not_held` when the account did not hold the Fleet then, 404 for an unknown Fleet and 400 for a bad report. Clause 10.4 does not refund HC credited this way.

## Uptime and the downtime credit (clause 4.4)

Clause 4.4 credits, from our own records, every hour of a Booking touched by a period of more than 15 consecutive minutes in which the Fleet was unavailable or unreachable. A Fleet is unavailable only when its machines, or our network to them, fail, not because of software. The probes below ask the hub's software, so they also record periods in which only software failed, and those are credited too: more than the clause requires. O5 (`docs/FLEET_API_ISSUES.md`) replaces them with probes of the machines and the network. These are the records.

- **Probes.** Every `SPARKPIPE_PAY_HEALTH_SECONDS` (30) seconds the live service asks the hub of each serving Fleet, over its tunnel, for two read-only answers (10-second timeout each), and makes no other call: `GET /health`, where the chat layer asks each configured model's engine and lists each one's `status`, and `GET /v1/sparkpipe/status`, the chat layer's public view of its model control (`state` of `idle`, `swapping` or `failed`, and `models` with the resident one marked `active`). `/health` answers 503 whenever any configured model is not loaded, which on a Fleet that keeps one model resident is nearly always, so its overall answer is not used. Fleets that share a hub share one probe. The Fleet is up when:
  - a swap is under way (`state: swapping`): requests wait for the model, they do not fail;
  - otherwise, every resident model's `/health` entry has status 200;
  - with no model resident, the control is `idle`, so the next request loads one; after a `failed` swap with nothing resident it is down;
  - on a hub without model control (`/v1/sparkpipe/status` answers 404), every configured model answers (`ok: true`);
  - when the model control does not answer, at least one model answers.

  It is down when `/health` does not answer at all.
- **Down periods.** A Fleet is down from its last good probe until its next one: the first failed probe opens a period that starts at the last good probe, and the next good probe closes it. A time in which the service took no probe, because it was stopped or could not run, is a period too: when the next probe comes more than 90 seconds (or three probe intervals) after the last good one, the gap is recorded with `cause: gateway`, since customers could not reach the Fleet through the gateway either. So a period always starts a little early and ends a little late. Periods of one Fleet that at most one probe interval separates (`SPARKPIPE_PAY_HEALTH_SECONDS`), such as two periods split by a single good probe, are one period for the credit: `Uptime.periods` joins them under the first period's id before the credit judges them, so a flapping Fleet is credited for the whole time it could not be used, and the status page lists each recorded period with `creditable` true when its joined period is over 15 minutes. A joined period that grows after a pass is credited again for the hours it adds.
- **Records.** `uptime/state.json` keeps each Fleet's last good probe, the time of its first record, the open period and the last probe's detail; each closed period is a line (`span`, `fleet`, `start`, `end`, `cause`) of `uptime/<YYYY-MM>.jsonl`, by the month it ended. They hold no account or request data, and are kept five years, like the ledger. `sparkpipe_pay.py uptime FLEET FROM TO` lists a Fleet's periods in a window, for checking a customer's own records under the second sentence of clause 4.4; a claim that our records do not cover is credited with `credit EMAIL HC NOTE`.
- **Credit.** A period qualifies once it has lasted more than 15 minutes (`OUTAGE`). It is credited in passes: as soon as it qualifies (or at its end, if it was already closed when the service saw it), again each 24 hours while it lasts, and at its end. Each pass credits every booked hour from the hour the period started through the hour it has reached (the hour in which it ended, or the current hour), at the Rate of the Booking that holds it: one `hc_lot` per account with `source: downtime`, the period as `ref`, and the Fleet and `hours`, then `downtime.credited` with the hours, the HC and `clause: 4.4` (always emailed), then a `downtime` line for the pass. Each booked hour is credited once, whatever number of periods touch it. Hours under announced maintenance are credited by the maintenance instead (clause 4.3), never twice. We are emailed (`SPARKPIPE_PAY_NOTIFY`) when a period first qualifies and when it ends, with each account credited. The lots are written before the pass's `downtime` line, so a service that stops in between credits only the remaining hours when it starts again.
- **Status page.** `GET /status` answers, with no sign-in and no account data, `now`, `outageMinutes` (15) and, for each Fleet of the Fleet List, `status` (`up`, `down`, `maintenance`, `not_serving`, or `unknown` before its first record), `since`, `recordsSince`, `uptime` (the percentage of the last 24 hours, 7, 30 and 90 days, from its first record, leaving out announced maintenance; null before any record), `incidents` (every period of the last 90 days, newest first, with `start`, `end`, `minutes`, `cause` and `creditable`: over 15 minutes) and the announced `maintenance`. `site/status.html` and `site/status-ko.html` show it.
- **Sandbox.** The sandbox takes no probes. `POST /market/sandbox/fleet {"fleet", "up": false}` opens a period at the sandbox clock, and `up: true` closes it, so the credit can be tried with the sandbox clock.

## Failover (clause 15.7)

If a Fleet that a customer holds is unavailable, clause 15.7 lets us give the customer the use of another Fleet that is free, without its working state. The gateway does that by itself whenever the probes below find the Fleet down, whatever the cause.

- **When.** A Fleet is down while its uptime records hold an open period: a failed probe, or a connection to its hub that the gateway could not open. A request whose hub refuses the connection marks the Fleet down at once and is sent again, before any of its body is read, to the Fleet below.
- **Which Fleet.** The first Fleet in Fleet List order, other Fleets before Hour Fleets, that is serving, up by its own probes (in the sandbox: not taken down), not booked or under maintenance now, and not serving another account's failover in the current hour. The choice is a `failover` ledger line (`failover`, `account`, `from`, `to`, `start`), and the account keeps that Fleet while the failover lasts.
- **Requests.** They go to the free Fleet's hub as that Fleet (`X-SparkPipe-Fleet`), with `X-SparkPipe-Failover-From` naming the held Fleet; the answer carries `X-SparkPipe-Failover-From` too, and the sandbox's simulated replies add `failover_from` to `sparkpipe`. The account's working state stays on the held Fleet, so the first requests on the free Fleet prefill again. The connection is closed 30 seconds after the hold of the held Fleet ends or, if sooner, 30 seconds after a booking or maintenance of the free Fleet starts.
- **End.** A `failover_end` line (`end`, `reason`) ends it as soon as the held Fleet serves again (`recovered`), when a booking or maintenance of the free Fleet starts (`taken`: bookings always come first), when the account's hold ends (`hold_ended`), or when the free Fleet goes down (`down`). While the held Fleet stays down, the next request takes another free Fleet if there is one; if none is free, the gateway answers 503 `fleet_down`.
- **Holder schedule.** The free Fleet's schedule names the account for every hour its failover touches, so that Fleet's hub admits it and later deletes its working state under clause 6.2 like any holder's.
- **Notice.** `fleet.failover` (`state` `started` or `ended`, `from`, `to`, `start`, `end`, `reason`), emailed by default, tells the customer where it is served and that its working state is not there.
- **Credits.** Failover does not reduce the downtime credit: every booked hour of the held Fleet that a period of more than 15 minutes touches is still credited (clause 4.4), and maintenance hours are still credited under clause 4.3.

## The holder schedule

`GET /market/fleets/<fleet>/holders?from=<slot>&to=<slot>` lets the hub see who holds each hour of a Fleet, so the engine can delete working state when another customer's booking starts or 24 hours after the last held hour (clause 6.2). It answers `{"fleet": "F1A", "now": "<stamp>", "hours": [{"start": "<slot>", "account": "AC..."}]}`: every hour in `[from, to)` held by a booked Week, an HC-booked Hour or a failover to that Fleet (above), in order, without the hours of announced maintenance. The hub polls it every 30 seconds for the last 25 hours through the next 25; it reads only the bookings that overlap the window.

It is authenticated with `Authorization: Bearer` and the upstream key (`SPARKPIPE_POOL_KEY_FILE`), compared in constant time: the hub already holds that key to call the Fleets, and no other service credential exists. It answers 401 for a missing or wrong key (and whenever no upstream key is configured), 404 for an unknown Fleet, and 400 for a missing or malformed `from` or `to`, `to` not after `from`, or a window over 7 days. It is not rate limited.

## availability.txt

`https://sparkpipe.ai/availability.txt` (clause 4.2) had no server. The live service generates it on every request, and `deployment/vps/sparkpipe-pay.conf` maps the public path to `http://127.0.0.1:18090/availability.txt`:

```text
# SparkPipe availability, generated 2027-01-06T03:00:00Z, Customer Agreement clause 4.2
# fleet serving status (hour-fleet, pinned or available)
F1A serving pinned
F1B serving hour-fleet
waitlist locks 2
```

The last line is the number of accounts that hold a waitlist lock, the same number as `waitlist.locks` in the public state.

## Sandbox

`/pay/sandbox/api` runs the same service with `SPARKPIPE_PAY_SANDBOX=1`: every Fleet is serving, `hoursFrom` and `weeksFrom` do not apply, `/market/buy` issues HC at once, `/market/sandbox/clock` moves the market clock forward by whole hours (rounds, lapses, maintenance and downtime credits catch up), `/market/sandbox/fleet` takes a Fleet down and brings it back, `/market/sandbox/reset` starts an empty ledger, chat replies are simulated, Toss Payments runs in test mode, no mail is sent and the sign-in code is returned in the answer.
